# Where to open a coffee shop in New York City

Data as of 2026-11-10. 3,252 coffee shops, 197 neighbourhoods, 428 subway stations.

## Recommendation

### 1. Downtown Brooklyn-DUMBO-Boerum Hill (Brooklyn): 79/100

Downtown Brooklyn‑DUMBO‑Boerum Hill already supports a thriving café scene, with 53 coffee shops (including well‑known chains like Starbucks, Dunkin and Devocion) and a steady flow of about 2,064 weekday subway riders per shop, thanks to 111,457 daily subway entries across eight nearby stations. The area’s 800 storefronts—only 43 of which are vacant—mean you’ll have plenty of location options, and the median resident income of roughly $44,300 gives you a solid base of spending power. Recent growth (14 new coffee shops in the past two years) and an overall neighborhood score of 79/100 highlight both strong foot traffic and a supportive market for new cafés. One thing to watch: because the neighborhood is a work‑area hub, business peaks during weekday mornings to late afternoons and can dip noticeably on weekends, so plan staffing and inventory accordingly.

### 2. Midtown South-Flatiron-Union Square (Manhattan): 79/100

Midtown South‑Flatiron‑Union Square already supports 106 coffee shops—about half are chains like Starbucks, Dunkin and Gregory’s Coffee—yet there are still 101 vacant storefronts, so you’ll have options for a good location. On a typical weekday the nine nearby subway stations see 308,289 entries, which works out to roughly 2,881 subway riders for every coffee shop, giving you a steady stream of potential customers during the busy workday. The neighborhood’s median resident income of about $65,900 and the overall score of 79/100 show strong spending power and a growing café scene. The main thing to watch is the very high rent that many New York locations command, so be sure to budget carefully.

### 3. Midtown-Times Square (Manhattan): 79/100

Midtown‑Times Square already supports 224 coffee shops, and each one draws about 1,840 weekday subway riders, so the foot traffic is strong enough to keep a new café busy. With 2,744 storefronts in the area and only 181 vacant, there are still openings, and the median resident income of roughly $66 K means locals have solid spending power. The neighborhood’s café scene is growing—43 new shops have opened in the past two years—so customers are accustomed to trying new spots. Just be aware that rents in this prime location are very high, so keeping costs under control will be crucial.

### 4. SoHo-Little Italy-Hudson Square (Manhattan): 79/100

SoHo‑Little Italy‑Hudson Square already supports 91 coffee shops—most of them independent—yet each shop still draws about 938 weekday subway riders, and the area sees 86,296 subway entries each weekday across just six stations, giving you a steady flow of potential customers. With 1,652 storefronts and only 61 vacant, there’s still space to set up, and the median resident income of roughly $79,000 means locals have solid spending power for a good cup of coffee. The neighborhood’s overall score of 79 highlights strong foot traffic, a growing café scene, and an independent‑friendly vibe, all of which are encouraging for a first‑time owner. Just keep an eye on the very high rents and the dense competition—those are the biggest challenges you’ll need to manage.

### 5. Upper East Side-Carnegie Hill (Manhattan): 78/100

Upper East Side‑Carnegie Hill already has a strong coffee‑shop market, with 44 cafés (20 chains and 24 independents) serving nearly 3,000 weekday subway riders per shop and a total of 133,008 subway entries across six nearby stations, so you’ll have plenty of foot traffic. The neighborhood also offers 1,381 storefronts, only 48 of which are vacant, and residents earn a median income of about $71,933, giving you a solid base of spending power. All existing cafés have earned an A grade and the overall area score is 78/100, highlighting a healthy, growing café scene. The one thing to watch is the level of competition—there are already many established coffee shops and six new ones have opened in the past two years, so you’ll need a clear concept to stand out.

## Operations Analyst

Well‑run cafés tend to keep their health‑grade scores high—about 95 % of all NYC coffee shops earn an A, and the rate is even higher for chains (98 %) than for independents (94 %). The cafés that get shut down are usually those with serious sanitation lapses, especially surfaces that aren’t cleaned and sanitised properly, a problem identified at 1,869 locations. When an inspection does lead to a closure, two‑thirds of the time it’s because of pest‑related issues such as mice, roaches, flies or gaps that let pests in.

- 95% of NYC coffee shops hold an A health grade.
- Chains: 98% A grades; independents: 94%.
- 4% of coffee shops were temporarily closed by the Health Department at least once in the last few years.
- 66% of the 119 coffee shops closed by inspectors had a pest problem (mice, roaches, flies or gaps that let pests in) at that inspection.
- Most common serious problem: surfaces not cleaned and sanitised properly (found at 1,869 coffee shops).
