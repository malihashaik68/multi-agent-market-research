# Where to open a coffee shop in New York City

Data as of 2026-11-19. 3,256 coffee shops, 197 neighbourhoods, 428 subway stations.

## Recommendation

### 1. Midtown South-Flatiron-Union Square (Manhattan): 79/100

Midtown South‑Flatiron‑Union Square already supports a thriving café scene—there are 106 coffee shops (about half independent) and 28 new ones opened in the past two years, showing strong demand, and 95 % of them earn an A grade, which signals a market that values quality. With roughly 2,881 weekday subway riders per shop and a total of 308,289 subway entries across nine stations, foot traffic is high, and the median resident income of about $65,900 gives you a solid base of spending power. You’ll also find plenty of storefront options, since 1,665 retail spaces exist and only 101 are vacant, making it easier to locate a spot that fits your concept. The main caution is the very high rent in this area; be sure your financial plan can absorb the premium cost before you sign a lease.

Downtown Brooklyn‑DUMBO‑Boerum Hill already draws a lot of people—each coffee shop nearby sees about 2,064 weekday subway riders, and the whole area gets over 111,000 subway entries a day, giving you steady foot traffic from workers who are out from 8 am to 4‑8 pm. With 800 storefronts and only 43 vacant, there’s still space to choose a good location, and the median resident income of roughly $44,300 means locals have solid spending power. All existing cafés have an A health grade and the overall neighborhood score is 79/100, highlighting a thriving café scene that’s attracting new shops (13 have opened in the past two years). The main thing to watch is the competition: there are already 53 coffee shops (16 chains and 37 independents), so you’ll need a clear concept or niche to stand out.

Upper East Side‑Carnegie Hill already has a strong café culture, with 44 coffee shops (20 chains and 24 independents) all earning A‑grade health scores, and the area sees about 2,956 subway riders each weekday per shop—so there’s plenty of foot traffic from commuters who arrive early and stay through the workday. ## Competitor Analyst

## Operations Analyst

Well‑run cafés tend to keep clean, sanitized surfaces and stay on top of pest control, which is why the vast majority earn an A health grade—about 95 % citywide, 98 % for chains and 94 % for independents. When inspectors do find serious issues, they most often cite surfaces that aren’t properly cleaned, and about two‑thirds of the shops that were shut down had a pest problem such as mice, roaches or flies. In short, consistent cleaning and preventing pests are the key habits that keep a coffee shop out of trouble.

- 95% of NYC coffee shops hold an A health grade.
- Chains: 98% A grades; independents: 94%.
- 4% of coffee shops were temporarily closed by the Health Department at least once in the last few years.
- 66% of the 119 coffee shops closed by inspectors had a pest problem (mice, roaches, flies or gaps that let pests in) at that inspection.
- Most common serious problem: surfaces not cleaned and sanitised properly (found at 1,875 coffee shops).
